The problem

One contract, tracked the way it is today.

A real sequence. Nothing goes wrong procedurally — the file is stored correctly, the team does its job. The money is still lost.

DAY 0

Agreement signed

Both sides read all 34 pages. Terms are negotiated carefully.

DAY 1

Filed to the shared drive

Two dates get copied into a calendar. The other 19 obligations don't.

DAY 46

The person who signed it moves on

Context lived in one head. It leaves with them.

DAY 112

Delivery arrives 6 days late

Ops logs the delay, chases the vendor, moves on. No one opens the contract.

DAY 118

Invoice paid in full

The 2% late-delivery credit is never claimed. The 30-day claim window closes.

Read once, at the wrong time

Clauses are reviewed during negotiation, but they only become relevant months later — when nobody is reading.

Nobody owns it full-time

A 60-person distributor can't justify a contracts hire, so tracking falls to whoever closed the deal, on top of their real job.

Enterprise tools don't fit

Full contract lifecycle platforms are priced and scoped for legal departments that these companies don't have.

How it works

From a filed PDF to a monitored obligation.

STEP 1 · LIVE

Add documents

Drag in PDFs and Word files. Each one is parsed page by page so answers can point back to a page you can turn to.

STEP 2 · LIVE

Ask in plain language

"What do we get if Acme is late?" Answers come only from your documents, and cite the document and page behind each one.

STEP 3 · NEXT

Obligations are extracted

Each clause becomes a structured record — type, trigger, value, party, date — with a link back to its page.

STEP 4 · NEXT

Reviewed once, then stored

Low-confidence items are flagged for a person to confirm. After that the workspace is the record.

STEP 5 · NEXT

Alerts, then a draft letter

You're notified before a window closes, with a claim or notice letter ready to review and send.

Digital PDFs and Word files are read directly. Scanned pages need OCR, which is not in this build.

    What the model actually does

    Seven jobs, none of them magic.

    Reads legal phrasing

    "Notwithstanding", "provided that", "save as set out in Schedule 3" — conditional language that keyword search cannot resolve.

    Turns clauses into records

    A paragraph becomes a typed row with a trigger, a value, a party and a date that a calendar and a query can act on.

    Connects related documents

    Links the purchase order to the master agreement that governs it, and the amendment that changed the delivery window.

    Retrieves the exact clause

    Answers are assembled from specific passages, not summarised impressions of a document.

    Answers questions as asked

    Operations staff ask "can we still claim this?" — not "SELECT * FROM obligations".

    Writes the follow-up

    Claim notices and renewal letters drafted with the real dates, amounts and clause references already filled in.

    Keeps its work checkable

    Every answer carries a citation to the document and page it came from, and citations that cannot be matched to a real page are withheld rather than shown. Anything the documents don't cover comes back as "not covered" — not as a plausible guess.
